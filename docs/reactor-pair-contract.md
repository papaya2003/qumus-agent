# Two-flake reactor integration contract

Owner clarification, 2026-10-27: each experiment reacts exactly two small,
stacked 2D flakes on one narrow carrier chip. A successful reaction produces
one small product flake. This is not multi-nucleus CVD film growth. Chip size
and flake size are different quantities. Existing recordings remain immutable
historical fixtures and do not establish this new experimental definition.

## Ownership and boundary

The reactor line owns the devices, safety envelopes, loading/insertion,
whole-assembly XY motion, jobs, result acceptance and ledger lineage. The
material line owns stable input geometry, overlap geometry, reaction state,
single-product morphology and synthetic image formation. Neither line claims
validated chemical kinetics, real material identity, yield or measured quality.

The following ledger contract is independent of the material algorithm's
polygon representation. Geometry and material-state dictionaries are passed
through to the material backend, not regenerated by the reactor workflow.
The reactor-owned normalization adapter binds the material line's pure API.

## Input and persistence

- A carrier remains a sample of kind `chip`, with `reactor_substrate=true`.
- `chip.props.reactor_pair` is a versioned preparation manifest:
  `schema_version=1`, `chip_id`, `input_flake_ids` (exactly two distinct IDs,
  ordered bottom then top), `preparation_source`, and `preparation_id`.
- Both IDs must identify existing samples of kind `flake` on that carrier.
  An extra flake on the carrier invalidates the preparation. Neither input
  may already be consumed by another reactor preparation.
- Each flake's existing props hold its `material`, `area_um2`, and the
  material module's serialized geometry/state. The reactor does not infer
  material identity from microscope colours or rewrite the geometry.
- `preparation_source` is `synthetic_fixture` for offline seeded specimens.
  It must not imply that a real person performed or verified preparation.
  Physical assembly and a human preparation-report UI are separate work.
- Jobs reserve the carrier, both input flake IDs and reactor port. All
  hardware access remains through SafetyGate. Pair validation happens before
  exposure and again when recording the result.

## Observation and output

The material adapter supplies `experiment_kind=two_flake_reaction`,
`input_flake_ids`, `preparation_id`, `visible`, and a single optional `product`.
`product=None` means no verified product, not successful conversion. A
present product has one stable `id`, `material`, finite positive `area_um2`,
and backend-owned geometry/state. Its `source_flake_ids` equal the two input
IDs, and its area is bounded by their overlap (validated by the material
module) and by each input's area (validated again at the workflow boundary).

Result metrics describe this local pair/product, e.g. reaction fraction or
retained product area. Full-chip/FOV coverage is not a completion goal.
Visibility and execution success are distinct from a verified product. A
completion result must cite its successful reaction job, preparation ID,
fresh observation and product ID. Interrupted history remains uncertain;
a new image cannot silently certify the interrupted exposure.

The carrier's `reactor_result` stores the latest observation and evidence-job
ID. A verified product is a sample of kind `flake`, located on the same
carrier, with `source_flake_ids`, `reactor_preparation_id`,
`reactor_evidence_job_id`, `image_source=simulated` and the material payload.
Source flakes remain in the historical ledger with a consumed status and
preparation reference; they are never deleted. Replaying/re-submitting the
same successful result must not create more products. A changed preparation
or uncertain observation cannot reuse prior success.

Acceptance locks the SQLite writer before any goal, latest-job, preparation or
observation read. Product insertion, both source-consumption updates, project
acceptance and their events commit as one transaction, or all roll back. This
also prevents two independent connections from accepting different product IDs
for the same preparation. Hardware-job serialization alone is insufficient.

## Material adapter

`reactor_pair_backend.PairMaterialBackend` calls
`material_view.assembly_from_manifest` with the ordered ledger rows, each
carrying `props.material_placement=Placement.model_dump(mode="json")`.
`LocalReaction` snapshots are keyed by carrier in `reactor_state.pair_models`
and include the immutable preparation/assembly ID. Reusing that key with a
changed assembly or profile is rejected instead of resetting exposure.
Real lab-process restart binds the existing store without re-seeding samples;
consumed pairs and the single accepted product remain intact. Reactor device
identity and layout signature are checked before any checkpoint overlay.
Incompatible enable/disable or layout changes require a fresh explicitly named
simulation DB (or the original compatible configuration); old bytes and ledger
are preserved. New preparations use the restored world's seed.
Plant clock integration supplies the explicit synthetic `active` flag;
camera reads do not advance time. The activation temperature/flow thresholds
are fixtures, not chemical rate laws.

`material_view.observe_pair` produces a rich view. The adapter keeps it for
rendering and maps only the declared fields into strict `PairObservation`.
The product's entire view (including geometry, state and model version) is
preserved under `ReactionProduct.material_state`. `status=complete`, focus,
full-product visibility and a non-null product are all required for acceptance.
Failed/partial `render_product` geometry never substitutes for a verified
product. The camera caption explicitly reports "no verified product".

Everyday integrated benches use `two_flake_reaction` with empty carriers.
`prepare_reactor_pair` binds two physically transferred catalogued inputs;
`assess_reactor_inputs` and `get_reactor_pair_candidates` support optical pickup
isolation before irreversible transfer. The first preparation requires no
reaction goal; setting the pair goal follows successful preparation. Explicit
`synthetic_prepared_pairs` remains available for labelled mechanical fixtures.
An explicit `legacy_field_growth` configuration
exists for old acceptance fixtures; pair benches refuse that job and goal.
The new default requires the material modules to be integrated, and fails
explicitly if they are absent. It never silently falls back to film growth.

## Integration locations

- Preparation/result validation: `qumus2/records/reactor_pairs.py`.
- Job and sample reservations: `qumus2/flows/library/reactor.py`.
- Agent query/goal/result tools: `qumus2/agents/tools/reactor.py`.
- Physical world and optics bridge: `qumus2/testbench/reactor_devices.py`.
- Strict material normalization: `qumus2/testbench/reactor_pair_backend.py`.
- Pair state persists with the simulated world; public run evidence persists
  in sample props and events, and therefore in recording exports.
- The material line supplies its own importable model module and tests. The
  concrete adapter binding is an explicit integration point, not a duplicated
  reactor-side growth model.

## Acceptance intent

1. Normal preparation, load, insert, local reaction observation, cool, retract
   and return: demonstrate one pair and at most one product with lineage.
2. Uncertain exposure or ambiguous preparation: demonstrate a stopped/parked
   experiment, disabled targets and an honest need for review; do not fabricate
   a successful product or repeat irreversible exposure automatically.

`scripts/record_reactor_pair.py` implements `normal` and `camera-loss`. The
latter makes the microscope unavailable only after heating, verifies disabled
targets and retained uncertainty, and deliberately ends awaiting review.
It does not claim automatic recovery from an uncertain exposure. Existing
placement-correction tests continue to cover the bounded mechanical recovery.

All new recordings carry versioned directories, scenario purpose, synthetic
provenance and explicit outcome checks. Historical coverage-growth recordings
are retained as historical evidence only.

## Final process disposition

New Reactor Task Plan steps declare `reactor_outcome`: chip ID, `cold_storage` or
`observation_hold`, heater target (null for off), gas-flow target and rationale.
A flow writes `reactor.process` from measured controller feedback after its last
motion/control command. Completion requires a successful chip/project-bound job
and fresh evidence; later control/motion invalidates prior readings. Cold storage
requires targets off, measured access limits and the carrier in a storage slot.
Observation hold requires explicit targets, stabilized feedback and a verified
carrier in the port. Product acceptance cannot substitute for either condition.

`recover_reactor_load` resumes a parked cold load under the original reservations
and bounded recovery budget. It cannot repeat uncertain reaction exposure.
`inspect_reactor_product` transports the cold carrier through shared storage,
checks the same product with the ordinary microscope and returns it to storage.
